## Purpose

Set clear rules for honest, safe, respectful work. Protect the people we serve, the integrity of
peer support, and each other.

---

## Who this applies to

Every employee, contractor, student, intern and volunteer of Prayers of Care Inc — **and the
CEO / Owner.** An ethics code that the owner does not sign is not a code, it is a staff rule.

---

## Standards of conduct

### 1. Person-centered respect
- Honour each person's choice, dignity, culture and identity.
- **Do not pressure anyone toward any belief.** Prayers of Care Inc offers no faith-based
  activity. Spirituality is a domain a person may choose; follow their lead and never introduce
  religious content unless they ask. See MVV-01, Statement on Faith.
- Respect every recovery pathway equally, including medication for a mental health or substance
  use condition. Discouraging a person's prescribed medication is a violation of this Code.
- Use inclusive language. Never use "non-compliant" or "resistant" about a person exercising a
  choice.

### 2. Honesty and documentation integrity
- Tell the truth, in conversation, in the record and on a claim.
- **Never document a service that did not happen as documented.** Never alter a record to
  conceal an error. Never bill for a service not delivered as documented.
- Correct an error by amendment, dated and initialled — never by erasing, overwriting or
  back-dating.
- **Do not claim CARF accreditation until it is awarded.** "Preparing for CARF accreditation" is
  the accurate description until then. See JD-01 section 5.
- Admit mistakes and fix them. **Reporting your own error is not a disciplinary matter** — hiding
  one is.

### 3. Confidentiality and privacy
- Share information only with valid written authorization or where the law requires it.
- Protect paper and electronic records. Nothing containing a person's information is left in a
  vehicle, on a screen, or in an open bag.
- No conversation about a person served in a public place, a corridor, a car with anyone else
  present, or any online space.
- **Never confirm or deny that any individual receives services here** — not to a caller, not to
  a family member, not to anyone, without authorization. Take a number and route it to the
  Privacy Officer.

### 4. Peer role integrity
- Use lived experience to help the person, not to centre yourself.
- Follow your written disclosure plan and review disclosure in supervision.
- **Dual relationships are expected in a small recovery community and are not misconduct.
  Failing to disclose one is.** Disclose in writing by the next business day; your supervisor
  writes the management plan.
- The full boundary rules are in **JD-02 section 8** and are not repeated here, so the two can
  never disagree.

### 5. Money and gifts
- No lending, borrowing or giving money to or from a person served, in any amount, for any
  reason, ever.
- No gift beyond a token of nominal value. A gift that creates obligation is refused kindly and
  reported to your supervisor.
- No business or financial arrangement with a person served. Never hold anyone's cash, card,
  benefits or bank access. Never act as a representative payee. Never co-sign anything.

### 6. Safety
- Follow emergency, infection-control and community-safety procedures.
- Report incidents, and any suspicion of abuse, neglect or exploitation, immediately.
- No weapons, violence or intimidation.
- **You are never required to enter or remain anywhere you believe is unsafe, and leaving is
  never grounds for discipline.**

### 7. Impairment and professionalism
- Do not work under the influence of alcohol or any impairing substance.
- **If your own recovery is under strain, tell your supervisor.** That conversation is supported,
  not punished — see JD-02 section 10. Working impaired is the violation; asking for help is not.
- Reliable attendance, respectful communication, and dress and conduct suited to community
  settings.

### 8. Non-discrimination and non-retaliation
- No discrimination on any basis protected by law.
- **No retaliation** against anyone — staff or person served — for raising a concern in good
  faith, filing a grievance, or taking part in an investigation, an audit or an accreditation
  survey. Retaliation is itself a violation of this Code and is investigated as one.

### 9. Conflicts of interest
- Disclose any actual, potential or perceived conflict in writing, before it can influence a
  decision.
- Never steer a person served toward a business you or your family benefit from.
- **This applies to the CEO.** Owner conflicts are recorded in the LEAD-01 Decision Log, since
  there is no board to abstain in front of.

### 10. Technology and social media
- Use agency systems for authorized work only.
- Never post information about a person served, never post photographs taken during service, and
  **never confirm or deny that any individual receives services here** — even if they have posted
  about it themselves.
- Do not accept or send friend or follow requests with a current person served on a personal
  account. Decline, and mention it at your next supervision.
- Do not respond publicly to an online review or comment from a person served; refer it to the
  CEO.

### 11. Mandatory reporting
This is the most legally serious duty in this Code, so it is stated here in full rather than
deferred to another document.

- **North Carolina requires reporting by any person who suspects** abuse, neglect or
  exploitation of a child, or of a disabled or older adult. Confirm the current statutory
  citation, the receiving agency and the required timeframe: `[FILL IN — confirm with counsel or
  the county DSS]`
- **You report on suspicion. You do not need proof, and you do not need permission.**
- Tell your supervisor immediately — and if your supervisor is unavailable, or is the subject of
  the concern, **make the report yourself and tell the CEO afterwards.** The report does not wait
  for a chain of command.
- The Compliance Officer confirms and records that every required external report was made.
- Reporting in good faith is protected. Nobody is disciplined for a report that turns out to be
  unfounded.

### 12. Cooperation with review
Cooperate fully and truthfully with any internal review, payer audit, licensure inspection or
accreditation survey. Do not conceal, alter or destroy a record because a review is coming.

## What happens when a concern is raised

1. Logged on receipt, including anonymous reports.
2. Investigated by someone **not involved in the matter**. Where the concern involves the CEO,
   the independent designated person above handles it.
3. A written finding, with any corrective action, an owner and a due date.
4. The reporter is told the outcome where they are identifiable.
5. Where a violation may affect a peer credential or a professional licence, the matter is
   reported to that credentialing body as its rules require.
6. Consequences run from coaching through to termination, applied consistently regardless of who
   the person is.

## Procedure

1. Receive this Code at hire, at any revision, and annually.
2. **Ask questions before signing.** Signing is not a formality; if something here is unclear or
   you disagree with it, say so first.
3. Sign the acknowledgment.
4. Signed copies are filed in the personnel file and tracked below.
