## Purpose

LEAD-01 says leadership meets **at least monthly**, keeps an agenda and written minutes, and
tracks actions to closure. This template is how that actually happens. It turns the promise in
the policy into a piece of paper a surveyor can hold.

Prayers of Care Inc has **no board of directors**. That makes these minutes more important here
than they would be at a larger agency, not less: together with the **Decision Log in LEAD-01
section G**, they are the entire written record that the corporation is being governed by
someone. If a month has no minutes, that month has no governance record.

---

## How to use this template — step by step

1. **Before the meeting.** Print or copy Part 1. Fill in the meeting date, time and place at the
   top. Add anything under item 8 that is specific to this month.
2. **Send it out.** Give the agenda to whoever is attending at least one day ahead, so nobody
   arrives cold.
3. **During the meeting.** Work down the agenda in order. One person writes in Part 2 as you go
   — not from memory afterwards.
4. **Start with last month's actions.** Part 2 section C is first for a reason. Every meeting
   opens by asking what happened to the things you said you would do.
5. **Every action gets a name and a date.** An action with no owner and no due date is not an
   action, it is a wish. The columns are there to stop that.
6. **Decide what goes in the Decision Log.** Before you close the meeting, look at section E and
   the rule in section H below. Anything that qualifies gets copied into LEAD-01 section G the
   same day.
7. **After the meeting.** Send the draft minutes to everyone who attended. Give them a short
   correction period, then finalise, sign in section I, and file per section J.
8. **File it.** Same folder, same naming, every month. Minutes you cannot find at survey are
   minutes you did not keep.

> **Do not write names of persons served in these minutes.** Leadership minutes are an
> administrative record, not a clinical one, and they get handed to surveyors, auditors and
> sometimes payers. Refer to a person by **initials or record number** — "incident involving
> client #____" — and keep the detail in the incident report or the service record where it
> belongs. This is one of the easiest findings in the manual to hand a surveyor for free.

## What a surveyor will ask

| They ask | They are testing | What has to be true |
|---|---|---|
| "Show me twelve months of leadership meeting minutes." | LEAD-01 B.1 — that leadership meets, not just exists | Twelve files, in order, no silent gaps. A month not held is recorded and explained |
| "Pick this one. What did you decide, and what happened next?" | Whether actions close | The decision in section E, the action in section F, and the same action closed in section C of a later month |
| "You have no board. Where is the record of your major decisions?" | 1.A governance for an owner-operated corporation | The LEAD-01 Decision Log, with entries traceable back to these minutes via section H |
| "What does your quality data show, and what did you change?" | That data reaches leadership and changes something | A number in section D, then a decision or action that came from it |
| "Was there a meeting you missed?" | Honesty and self-monitoring | The not-held line, completed — far stronger than an unexplained gap |
| "This action has been open for five months." | LEAD-01 B.4 | It was re-assigned or dropped at meeting three, and the minutes say which |
| "Who chairs when the meeting is about you?" | Independence, and 1.J | The independent person named in ETH-01 — and the minutes show it happening |
| Asked of a **peer specialist**: "how do problems you raise reach leadership?" | Whether the culture matches the policy | Their answer matches these minutes, and something they raised appears in one |

## Common ways minutes fail a survey

| The defect | Why it fails | What this template does about it |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes are an attendance list plus "discussed" | Shows a meeting happened, not that leadership led | Sections D, E and F force content, decisions and owners |
| Actions with no owner or no due date | Nothing can be tracked to closure | The Owner and Due columns are required before closing |
| Actions that roll over forever | Looks like leadership has no follow-through | The Meetings carried column and the three-meeting rule |
| Gaps of two or three months | Reads as leadership stopping | The not-held line makes the gap deliberate and explained |
| Decisions minuted but never logged | With no board, the governance record is incomplete | Section H, checked before the meeting closes |
| Names of persons served in the minutes | Confidentiality finding, handed over for free | The notice in "How to use this template" — initials or record number only |
| Minutes written weeks later from memory | Detail is gone, and it shows | Step 3 — written during the meeting |
| Minutes signed by someone who was not there | Undermines every record in the binder | The approval note in section I |
