# Mock Survey Interview Guide

Run this as a real rehearsal at least 60 days before the survey. Have someone who does not work in the program ask the questions. Write down the actual answers. Any answer that is a shrug, a guess, or "I'd have to ask the director" is a gap — fix it with training, not with a script.

**Tell every person the three rules before the survey:**
1. Tell the truth. A surveyor can tell when you are performing.
2. "I don't know, but I know where to find it" is a good answer. Guessing is not.
3. Never say what you think they want to hear. Say what actually happens.

## A. Interview with the {{CEO_TITLE}} / leadership
1. Walk me through your organizational chart. Who is accountable for quality?
2. What does your strategic plan say, and what data did it come from?
3. When did the governing body last review performance? Show me the minutes.
4. What are your top three risks and what are you doing about each?
5. Show me your budget and the assumptions behind it. Who reconciles the bank statement?
6. What did last year's performance analysis tell you, and what changed because of it?
7. How do you know your staff are competent, not just trained?
8. Tell me about a complaint you received and what happened.
9. What is your biggest weakness as an organization right now?

## B. Interview with the {{PROGRAM_DIRECTOR}}
1. Describe the program. Who is it for and what do you actually do?
2. How long from referral to first contact? How do you know?
3. What happens to someone you cannot serve?
4. What is your caseload cap and how did you decide on it?
5. Show me how a person-centered plan gets made. Who writes the goals?
6. How do you know notes are done on time?
7. What happens after a critical incident?
8. How do you handle it when a peer specialist knows a participant from their own recovery community?
9. What happens when someone just stops showing up?

## C. Interview with Peer Support Specialists (the most important interview)
1. What is peer support? What is it not?
2. What makes you qualified for this job?
3. How often do you get supervision, and what do you talk about?
4. Give me an example of when you shared your own story. Why did you share it?
5. What would you do if a participant asked you something you are not qualified to answer?
6. What do you do if someone tells you they stopped taking their medication?
7. You are at a home visit and you feel unsafe. Exactly what do you do?
8. Someone escalates in your car. What do you do?
9. You see a participant at your own recovery meeting. What now?
10. A participant sends you a friend request. What do you do?
11. Where are the emergency procedures? What do you do in a fire here?
12. Who do you report a suspected abuse situation to, and how fast?
13. How do you know what a participant's goal is?
14. How do you take care of yourself in this job? Does the agency help?
15. If you had a concern about the agency, who would you tell? What if it was about your boss?

## D. Interview with persons served
Never coach answers. Ask people who will speak freely, including people who have complained.
1. How did you get connected here?
2. How long did you wait?
3. Did someone explain your rights? Do you have a copy?
4. If you had a complaint, what would you do? Who would you call?
5. Would anything bad happen to you if you complained?
6. Whose goals are on your plan? Who picked them?
7. Can you show me your plan? Are those your words?
8. Can you say no to something here?
9. Has anyone pressured you about medication or about how you do your recovery?
10. Where do you meet your peer specialist?
11. What has changed in your life since you started?
12. Is there anything you needed that they could not help with?
13. Do you know what happens when services end?

## E. Interview with the Supervisor
1. How do you assess competency? Have you ever watched someone deliver service?
2. How often do you supervise, and what is documented?
3. How do you supervise self-disclosure?
4. What do you do when a boundary problem comes up?
5. What have you found in record reviews and what did you do about it?
6. What do you do when a staff member is struggling with their own recovery?

## F. Document requests to expect
Have these physically ready in the Evidence Binder before day one.
- Organizational chart, leadership job descriptions, leadership minutes (12 months)
- Governing body minutes (12 months), conflict of interest disclosures, board self-assessment, CEO evaluation
- Strategic plan + environmental scan + review minutes
- Input analysis report + surveys + "you said, we did" summary + proof of distribution
- Budget + assumptions + 12 months budget-to-actual + bank reconciliations + external financial review
- Risk management plan + risk register + insurance declarations
- Health and safety plan + 4 quarters of inspections + 12 months of drill logs (all shifts, one unannounced) + incident log + quarterly incident analyses
- 3-5 complete personnel files
- Technology plan + BAAs + backup restore test + access log + separation checklist
- Rights statement (posted), grievance procedure (posted), grievance log, one complete grievance file, annual rights review
- Accessibility plan (all 8 areas) + barrier survey + accommodation log + this year's progress
- Performance measurement plan + measure definition sheets + 4 quarterly dashboards
- Annual performance analysis report + PI project charters + proof of distribution
- Program description, staffing plan, community resource directory
- Referral log, waitlist log, referral-out log
- 5-8 records of persons served, open and closed
- 4 quarters of record reviews + corrective action tracker + summary reports
- Records retention schedule + certificates of destruction

## G. The five questions that most often expose a gap
1. **"Show me the data."** Policies are easy. Four quarters of real data is not.
2. **"What did you do about it?"** Finding a problem is half. Closing it is the standard.
3. **"How did the people you serve find out?"** Distribution of results is the most-missed requirement in Section 1.M.
4. **"Show me the drill on the evening shift."** Drills usually cover one shift only.
5. **"Read me a goal in the person's own words."** Goals written by staff, in staff language, are the most common Section 2.C finding.
